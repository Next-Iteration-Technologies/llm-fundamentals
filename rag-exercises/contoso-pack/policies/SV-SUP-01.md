# SV-SUP-01 Bottle suppliers and stock

## 1 Purpose
This document explains where Contoso Water buys its 19-litre bottles, how much stock each warehouse keeps, and how warehouse staff order new bottles.

It applies to warehouse staff and team leads in London, Utrecht and Cologne. It is an internal document and is not shared with clients or suppliers. Supplier names, stock levels and order sizes must not be given to clients.

The owner of this document is Lee Wong, Delivery Operations Manager, London. Questions about invoices from suppliers go to Jonas Becker, Finance Controller, Cologne.

## 2 Bottle suppliers
Contoso Water buys filled 19-litre bottles from three suppliers. Each supplier serves one or more countries.

- **ClearSpring** supplies the London warehouse (UK).
- **BlueRiver** supplies the UK and the Netherlands. In the UK, BlueRiver is the second supplier for London. In the Netherlands, BlueRiver supplies the Utrecht warehouse.
- **Alpenquell** supplies the Cologne warehouse (Germany).

Every supplier delivers sealed bottles of still water to the warehouse and takes empty bottles back for cleaning and refilling.

Warehouse staff order only from the supplier listed for their country. A warehouse must not order from a supplier in another country without approval from Lee Wong.

## 3 New supplier for the Netherlands
Contoso Water is choosing a supplier for the new client volume in the Netherlands. The new volume comes from the Northwind onboarding in Utrecht, which adds 6 floors of coolers.

The decision has not been made in this document. It will be recorded in the management minutes. Until the minutes confirm a decision, the Utrecht warehouse keeps ordering from BlueRiver as usual.

Staff in Utrecht who are asked about the new supplier should say that the choice is being made and will be in the management minutes. This document will be updated once the decision is final.

## 4 Stock levels in each warehouse
Each Contoso Water warehouse keeps enough full bottles to cover its weekly deliveries plus a safety margin.

- The minimum stock is 1 week of normal deliveries plus 20 percent.
- The maximum stock is 3 weeks of normal deliveries, because bottles take up a lot of space.
- Stock is counted every Friday afternoon after the last van returns.

Bottle cooler rental includes 4 bottles a week per cooler, so the weekly need is mostly the number of bottle coolers on the routes times 4, plus orders for extra bottles (WC-400).

If stock falls below the minimum, the warehouse team lead tells Lee Wong on the same day.

## 5 Ordering bottles
Each warehouse team lead places one regular order a week with the supplier for their country. The order is placed on Friday, after the stock count, for delivery to the warehouse early the next week.

The order is the number of bottles needed to bring stock back up to 2 weeks of normal deliveries.

- Extra orders are allowed when a large new client starts or when stock falls below the minimum.
- Any single order of more than 3 weeks of normal deliveries needs approval from Lee Wong.
- Orders are placed by email to the supplier, with a copy to Lee Wong.

## 6 Receiving deliveries from suppliers
When a supplier delivers bottles to a Contoso Water warehouse, a warehouse operative checks the delivery before signing.

- Count the bottles and compare with the order.
- Check that every bottle is sealed and has a best-before date at least 3 months away.
- Reject any bottle with a broken seal, a crack or a dent.

Bottles are moved on trolleys, at most 2 at a time. A 19-litre bottle weighs about 19 kg, so nobody carries more than one by hand.

Rejected bottles are noted on the delivery note and go back with the supplier's driver.

## 7 Empty bottles and returns
Empty bottles collected by Contoso Water drivers go back to the supplier for cleaning and refilling.

Drivers bring empty bottles back to the warehouse at the end of each route. Warehouse staff stack them in the returns area, separate from full stock. The supplier collects empties when it delivers the next order.

Damaged empty bottles are kept apart and marked, so the supplier can replace them. The number of empties returned each week is checked against the number delivered, and large gaps are reported to Lee Wong.

## 8 Supplier problems
If a supplier cannot deliver on time, or delivers bottles that are damaged or have a taste or smell problem, the warehouse team lead tells Lee Wong on the same day.

Lee Wong decides what to do. Options include:

- Moving stock between warehouses in the same country.
- Asking the second supplier in that country for a short-term order, where there is one.
- Telling the service desk to warn clients about a delay.

Problems with suppliers are recorded and reviewed every quarter by Lee Wong and Jonas Becker.
