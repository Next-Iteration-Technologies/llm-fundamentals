# HR-HRS-01 Working hours, overtime and breaks

## 1 Purpose

This policy explains the normal working hours at Contoso Water, how overtime is approved and paid, and which breaks employees must take. It applies to all employees in the United Kingdom, the Netherlands and Germany: office staff, warehouse staff and drivers.

The working week at Contoso Water is Monday to Friday. Deliveries to clients happen Monday to Friday between 07:00 and 16:00. Weekend work is rare and only happens when a manager asks for it, for example for an urgent leak or a large installation.

Where national law sets a stricter rule than this policy, the law always wins. Priya Shah, HR Manager, owns this policy.

## 2 Normal working hours

Normal full-time working hours at Contoso Water are:

- **Office staff:** 37.5 hours a week, 7.5 hours a day plus an unpaid lunch break. Most office staff work between 08:30 and 17:00. Everyone should be available between 10:00 and 15:00, the core hours.
- **Warehouse staff and drivers:** 40 hours a week, 8 hours a day plus an unpaid break.

Drivers and warehouse staff work in two shifts so that clients can be served from 07:00 to 16:00:

- **Early shift:** 06:30 to 15:00, with a 30-minute break.
- **Late shift:** 08:00 to 16:30, with a 30-minute break.

Lee Wong, Delivery Operations Manager, publishes the shift rota for London, Utrecht and Cologne in PeopleHub, the HR app, at least **2 weeks** in advance. If you need to swap a shift, find a colleague to swap with and ask your team lead to approve it in PeopleHub.

## 3 Recording your hours

Every employee at Contoso Water records their working time, so that overtime is paid correctly and nobody works too much.

- **Drivers** clock in and out on the delivery app on their company phone. The times go into PeopleHub automatically.
- **Warehouse staff** clock in and out on the tablet at the warehouse door.
- **Office staff** confirm their week in PeopleHub under **My hours** every Friday. You only need to change the hours if you worked more or less than normal.

Recording hours is required by law in Germany and is good practice in all three countries. Managers check the hours of their team each Monday.

If you forget to clock in or out, correct it in PeopleHub the same day and add a short note. Your manager approves the correction.

## 4 Overtime: how to get it approved

Overtime means working more than your normal daily or weekly hours. At Contoso Water, overtime must be **approved by your manager before you work it**. The only exception is an emergency, for example a driver stuck in traffic on the last delivery of the day.

How to get overtime approved:

1. Ask your manager in PeopleHub under **My hours > Request overtime**, with the date, the number of hours and the reason.
2. Your manager approves or refuses before the overtime starts, normally the same day.
3. In an emergency, call your manager, do the work, and enter the overtime in PeopleHub by the end of the next working day.

Overtime that was not approved and was not an emergency will not be paid. Managers should plan routes and work so that overtime is the exception, not the rule.

## 5 Overtime: how it is paid

Overtime is paid or given back as time off, depending on your job.

**Drivers and warehouse staff** are paid for approved overtime:

- Monday to Friday: **1.5 times** your normal hourly pay.
- Saturday, Sunday and public holidays: **2 times** your normal hourly pay.

**Office staff** get time off instead of payment. One hour of overtime gives one hour of time off. Take the time off within 3 months, agreed with your manager. Office staff on team lead level and above are expected to work some extra hours from time to time without payment, within the legal limits.

Example: Aisha, a driver in London, works 2 approved extra hours on a Thursday and 4 hours on a Saturday for an urgent installation. She is paid 2 × 1.5 = 3 hours plus 4 × 2 = 8 hours, so 11 hours at her normal hourly pay. It is paid on the next pay day.

## 6 Breaks and rest

Breaks keep everyone safe, especially drivers and warehouse staff who lift 19-litre bottles all day. At Contoso Water, every employee takes **at least a 30-minute break** on a full working day. The break is unpaid and must be taken away from the van or desk if possible.

The law in each country also sets minimum rules:

- **United Kingdom:** a 20-minute break if you work more than 6 hours, and at least 11 hours of rest between two working days.
- **Netherlands:** a 30-minute break if you work more than 5.5 hours, and at least 11 hours of rest between two shifts.
- **Germany:** a 30-minute break after 6 hours and 45 minutes after 9 hours, and at least 11 hours of rest between two working days.

Drivers should also take a short stop of a few minutes every 2 hours of driving.

## 7 Maximum hours

Nobody at Contoso Water should work too many hours. These limits apply:

- No employee may work more than **48 hours a week** on average, measured over 17 weeks. In the UK you may sign an opt-out, but Contoso Water does not ask anyone to do so.
- In **Germany**, nobody may work more than **10 hours on one day**, as German law requires.
- Drivers may not drive for more than **9 hours** in one day, including the drive to and from the warehouse.

If you are asked to work more than these limits, say no and tell HR. Managers who see that a team member regularly works long hours must talk to them and change the plan.
